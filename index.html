Your mixer, your starting point

Artisan KSM195.
Two bowls. Four right tools.

Use the big bowl for the work.
Keep the small bowl for a second job.

The Australian KSM195 is a 300 W tilt-head mixer with 10 speeds. The current AU listing includes 4.7 L and 2.8 L stainless steel bowls, a wire whisk, flex-edge beater, pastry beater, dough hook and pouring shield. Older packaging may describe the main bowl as 4.8 L; check the items supplied with your unit.

KSM195 specifications & inclusions ↗

4.7 L

Default for dough, pastry, batter, butter, mash and chicken. The recipe drawers name the bowl.

2.8 L

Useful for the listed small whipped-cream and egg-white recipes with the wire whisk. Do not assume flex-edge scraping or shield fit in this bowl.

≤2 kg

AU heavy-mixture ceiling, including water—not a target or an automatic double-batch allowance. Stiffness and the manual still govern.

5 min / 20 min

Our conservative maximum heavy run, including initial mixing, followed by a mixer rest. AU guidance says 5–6 minutes; stop sooner if it strains.

Model-manual correction: the KSM195-linked manual says not to use Stir/speed 1 to mix yeast dough. These recipes use speed 2 for initial yeast-dough mixing as well as hook kneading. Lock the lowered head and secure the bowl before starting; turn off and unplug before tool changes.

The supplied pastry beater handles cold butter, mash and shredded meat. A separate rigid flat beater is not assumed. The normal stainless bowl does not churn frozen desserts: an optional 5KSMICM freeze bowl replaces it. The AU 5KSMICM and KSMPRA listings support KSM195 compatibility; ownership is not assumed. Before the first turn

The tool matters.
The endpoint matters more.

Your hook develops gluten, flex-edge beater mixes and creams, pastry beater cuts in cold butter, and whisk traps air. The KSM195 supplies movement—not cooking, rolled sheets or refrigeration.

The recipe tells you where to start.
The mixture tells you when to stop.

These are modest home batches for the KSM195, not capacity tests. Check the manual supplied with your unit and stop for strain. Keep the 2 kg AU heavy-mixture ceiling distinct from bowl volume, and keep heavy mixtures at or below speed 2.

KitchenAid AU: heavy dough and model limits ↗

Dough hook

Bread, pizza and enriched dough. Develop strength at speed 2; rest or hand-finish if needed. Not for shortcrust pastry.

Flex-edge beater

Cream butter and combine batter in the main bowl. The flexible edge helps scrape; it does not make overmixing harmless. Not our tool for chicken.

Pastry beater

Cut cold butter into flour at speed 2. Mash cooked potatoes at 2. For shredded cooked meat, the model chart specifies speed 4.

Wire whisk

Whipped cream and egg whites. Start low, then increase. Stop at the right peak—or continue deliberately to make butter.

Yeast dough: speed 2, never faster. KitchenAid AU’s heavy-mixture guidance calls for 5–6 minutes running followed by 20 minutes rest. This guide uses at most 5 minutes per heavy run, including initial mixing; follow stricter model instructions. Stop immediately for strain, stalling or walking—speed is not a fix.

Pizza dough

An oil-rich tray pizza for a domestic oven—not the lean, high-heat Neapolitan dough from Ninety Seconds.

Makes one 30–35 cm pizza, 3–4 portions

Ingredients & method — Pizza dough

Allow time

1–2 hours, including rise and baking

Bowl: 4.7 L stainless steel bowl

Equipment: Mixer, flex-edge beater, dough hook, scales, covered bowl and pizza tray

The ingredients

  • bread flour 300 g
  • instant dried yeast 7 g
  • caster sugar 2 g
  • fine salt 5 g
  • lukewarm water 150 ml
  • olive oil 50 ml
  • extra oil 5 ml for bowl

The method

  1. Check your exact model’s batch limit. Keep dough-hook kneading at speed 2. Count preliminary heavy mixing toward a maximum 5-minute heavy-mixing run, then rest the mixer 20 minutes before continuing, or follow a stricter model manual. Stop immediately if the mixer strains; hand-finish rather than increasing speed.
  2. Fit the flex-edge beater in the 4.7 L bowl. Combine dry ingredients at speed 2 for 15 seconds. Add water and oil at speed 2; mix 30–60 seconds until shaggy and joined.
  3. Stop, fit hook. Knead at speed 2, 2–3 minutes until smoother, elastic and slightly tacky. Stop earlier if already developed; do not chase a dry dough.
  4. Oil bowl, cover; prove about 30–60 minutes until doubled (source's 15–30 minutes may be optimistic in a cool room).
  5. Gently deflate by hand, stretch/roll to tray; rest 10 minutes if it springs back. Add modest toppings. Bake 230°C conventional / 210°C fan, about 15–20 minutes, until crust is golden and base crisp and cooked. Cool 2 minutes before slicing.

The endpoint: Golden crust with a crisp, cooked base.

If it goes wrong

no rise: check yeast age, water warmth and room temperature; tight dough: rest before stretching; soggy centre: reduce wet toppings and preheat oven fully. Do not put unsupported raw dough directly on oven bars.

Sandwich bread (white tin loaf)

Start with the lower water quantity and add only enough to leave the dough lightly tacky.

Makes one approximately 21 × 11 × 7 cm loaf, 10–12 slices

Ingredients & method — Sandwich bread (white tin loaf)

Allow time

3–5½ hours, including two rises and full cooling; allow an extra 20-minute mixer rest if needed

Bowl: 4.7 L stainless steel bowl

Equipment: Mixer, flex-edge beater, dough hook, scales and approximately 21 × 11 × 7 cm loaf tin

The ingredients

  • bread flour 500 g
  • instant yeast 7 g
  • caster sugar 12 g
  • fine salt 5 g
  • lukewarm water 250–300 ml (start with 250)
  • olive oil 60 ml
  • optional milk powder 15 g
  • bowl/tin oil 10 ml

The method

  1. Check your exact model’s batch limit. Keep dough-hook kneading at speed 2. Count preliminary heavy mixing toward a maximum 5-minute heavy-mixing run, then rest the mixer 20 minutes before continuing, or follow a stricter model manual. Stop immediately if the mixer strains; hand-finish rather than increasing speed.
  2. Fit the flex-edge beater in the 4.7 L bowl. Combine dry ingredients at speed 2 for 15 seconds. Add 250 ml water and oil; mix at speed 2 for 45–60 seconds, adding remaining water in 5–10 ml increments only if dry flour remains.
  3. Stop and unplug to fit the hook. Knead at speed 2 for 2–3 minutes until smooth, springy and lightly tacky. If insufficiently developed, rest dough and mixer 20 minutes, then knead at speed 2 another 1–2 minutes, or finish by hand. Observe the model duty limit.
  4. Cover in oiled bowl; prove 60–90 minutes until doubled. Deflate by hand; shape a tight log and put in greased tin.
  5. Cover and prove 40–60 minutes until puffy and near the rim; a gentle finger dent should return slowly, not immediately.
  6. Bake 200°C conventional / 180°C fan for 25–35 minutes until golden, loaf sides firm and base sounds hollow. Turn out; cool completely, about 1–2 hours, before slicing. Brush warm crust with a little oil if a softer crust is desired.

The endpoint: Golden loaf with firm sides and a hollow-sounding base; cool fully before slicing.

If it goes wrong

dense loaf: allow fuller rise, do not add excess flour; collapsed top: reduce final proof; gummy slices: bake longer and cool fully.

Standard tools03 / 25

Rosemary focaccia

Oiled hands preserve this dough’s softness better than extra flour when stretching it into the tray.

Makes one approximately 20 × 30 cm tray, 6 portions

Ingredients & method — Rosemary focaccia

Allow time

2½–3¼ hours, including rest, two rises and baking

Bowl: 4.7 L stainless steel bowl

Equipment: Mixer, flex-edge beater, dough hook, scales and approximately 20 × 30 cm metal tray

The ingredients

  • bread flour 400 g
  • instant yeast 6 g
  • salt 7 g
  • caster sugar 12 g
  • warm water 260 ml
  • olive oil 30 ml in dough + 20 ml for tin/top
  • rosemary leaves 2 g
  • sliced garlic 5 g
  • flaky salt 2 g

The method

  1. Check your exact model’s batch limit. Keep dough-hook kneading at speed 2. Count preliminary heavy mixing toward a maximum 5-minute heavy-mixing run, then rest the mixer 20 minutes before continuing, or follow a stricter model manual. Stop immediately if the mixer strains; hand-finish rather than increasing speed.
  2. Fit the flex-edge beater in the 4.7 L bowl. Combine dry ingredients at speed 2 for 15 seconds; add water and 30 ml oil, then mix at speed 2 for 45 seconds until joined.
  3. Hook at speed 2 for 2–3 minutes to a soft, stretchy dough; some sticking is normal. Cover and rest 20 minutes, then give one set of gentle stretch-and-folds by hand.
  4. Cover in oiled bowl, prove about 60–90 minutes until doubled. Transfer to generously oiled tray; gently ease towards corners. Rest 10 minutes if resisting, then stretch again.
  5. Cover 25–40 minutes until airy and puffy. Dimple with oiled fingers; distribute remaining oil, rosemary, garlic and flaky salt.
  6. Bake 200°C conventional / 180°C fan for 25–30 minutes until golden top and cooked, golden underside. Cool 10 minutes on rack.

The endpoint: Golden top and cooked, golden underside; airy dough before baking.

If it goes wrong

sticky is expected; use oiled hands, not handfuls of extra flour. Tight stretching: rest. Pale wet base: use a metal tray and bake a little longer; shade exposed garlic if browning too quickly.

Brioche — overnight small loaf

Cold overnight dough is easier to shape, while short speed-2 runs protect the mixer during enrichment.

Makes one small approximately 21 × 11 cm loaf, 8 slices

Ingredients & method — Brioche — overnight small loaf

Allow time

13–17 hours or longer, including ≥8 hours chilling, proofing and cooling; 8–15 minutes active hook mixing in short runs plus 20-minute machine rests

Bowl: 4.7 L stainless steel bowl

Equipment: Mixer, flex-edge beater, dough hook, scales and approximately 21 × 11 cm loaf tin

The ingredients

  • plain flour 275 g
  • milk 65 ml
  • caster sugar 15 g
  • instant yeast 3.5 g
  • beaten egg 100 g
  • soft but not melting unsalted butter 115 g
  • fine salt 3 g
  • additional beaten egg 15 g for glaze

The method

  1. Check your exact model’s batch limit. Keep dough-hook kneading at speed 2. Count preliminary heavy mixing toward a maximum 5-minute heavy-mixing run, then rest the mixer 20 minutes before continuing, or follow a stricter model manual. Stop immediately if the mixer strains; hand-finish rather than increasing speed.
  2. In a jug, stir milk, yeast and sugar together; wait 10 minutes if checking yeast activity. In the 4.7 L bowl, use the flex-edge beater at speed 2 to combine this with egg, flour and salt for 45–60 seconds, forming a shaggy dough. Rest covered 20 minutes.
  3. Hook at speed 2 for 2 minutes. Add butter in three portions at 2, allowing roughly 1–2 minutes per portion until absorbed. Interrupt heavy mixing after at most 5 minutes including preliminary mixing, or sooner if the model manual requires; rest mixer 20 minutes before continuing. Keep dough covered; refrigerate it during breaks if warm or greasy.
  4. Continue in 2–3-minute runs at 2, with required rests, until satiny and stretchy and a small piece stretches into a thin translucent membrane. Allow roughly 8–15 minutes total active hook time, not one continuous run. Hand-finish if necessary. Never use source's speed 4.
  5. Cover, rise roughly 1–2 hours until doubled. Gently deflate/fold, cover and refrigerate at least 8 hours (overnight).
  6. Divide cold dough into three equal balls or braid; place in buttered tin. Cover, prove approximately 2–3 hours until doubled/puffy; cold dough may take longer. Glaze lightly with egg.
  7. Bake 170°C conventional / 150°C fan 25–35 minutes until deeply golden and centre set; extend baking if needed and tent top if darkening. Cool in tin 5–10 minutes, then rack completely.

The endpoint: Satiny dough stretches into a thin translucent membrane; finished loaf is deeply golden with a set centre.

If it goes wrong

greasy dough: butter/dough too warm; chill briefly. Dough tears: use rest and further gentle development, not higher speed. Slow final proof: wait for puffiness rather than baking to the clock.

Shortcrust pastry

Visible pea-size butter pieces and minimal water help keep this pastry tender.

Makes pastry for one 23 cm single-crust tart, about 390–410 g

Ingredients & method — Shortcrust pastry

Allow time

2–2½ hours, including ≥60 minutes chilling, 20–30 minutes lined-tin chilling and blind baking

Bowl: 4.7 L stainless steel bowl

Equipment: Mixer, pastry beater, scales, rolling pin, wrap, 23 cm tart tin, baking paper and baking weights

The ingredients

  • plain flour 250 g
  • cold cubed unsalted butter 125 g
  • fine salt 2 g
  • ice-cold water 35–50 ml
  • optional caster sugar 10 g for sweet pastry

The method

  1. Fit the supplied pastry beater in the 4.7 L bowl. Combine flour and salt/sugar at speed 2 for 10 seconds. Add cold butter and mix at speed 2 for about 30–60 seconds, only until coarse crumbs with some pea-size butter pieces remain. Watch the texture rather than insisting on a timer.
  2. At speed 2, add water in small increments for about 10–20 seconds total, stopping when a squeezed handful holds together. Finish pressing together by hand; do not knead smooth.
  3. Flatten to disc, wrap and chill at least 60 minutes. Roll 3 mm thick, line tin without stretching; chill another 20–30 minutes.
  4. For an unfilled shell, line with baking paper and baking weights. Bake 200°C conventional / 180°C fan, 15–20 minutes; remove weights/paper and bake 8–12 minutes more until base dry and golden. For filled pies/tarts, follow filling recipe's baking instructions instead.

The endpoint: Coarse crumbs before water; a squeezed handful holds together; baked shell has a dry golden base.

If it goes wrong

crumbly: add water 5 ml at a time; sticky/greasy: chill, not more vigorous mixing; shrinkage: avoid stretching and allow chilling. Never use dough hook for this pastry.

Chocolate chip cookies

Weigh the beaten egg and yolk separately for a practical small batch without guessing half an egg.

Makes approximately 12 cookies at 40 g dough each

Ingredients & method — Chocolate chip cookies

Allow time

35–65 minutes, including optional 20–30 minutes chilling and tray cooling

Bowl: 4.7 L stainless steel bowl

Equipment: Mixer, flex-edge beater, scales, lined baking trays and cooling rack

The ingredients

  • soft unsalted butter 100 g
  • brown sugar 75 g
  • caster sugar 62 g
  • vanilla extract 5 ml
  • beaten whole egg 25 g
  • egg yolk 9 g
  • plain flour 138 g
  • bicarbonate of soda 1.2 g
  • fine salt 1 g
  • chocolate chips/chunks 100 g

The method

  1. Combine butter/sugars on 2 30 seconds, then 4 for 2–3 minutes until pale and creamy; stop and scrape midway.
  2. Add egg/yolk and vanilla; 4 about 30–60 seconds until emulsified.
  3. Add flour, bicarb and salt at Stir for 15–30 seconds, just until no flour streaks. Add chocolate at Stir 5–10 seconds or fold by hand.
  4. Scoop 40 g portions; chill 20–30 minutes if soft. Place well apart on lined cool trays. Bake 180°C conventional / 160°C fan 11–14 minutes until edges golden and centres still soft but not liquid. Rest on trays 5 minutes then rack.

The endpoint: Golden edges and soft but set centres, not liquid.

If it goes wrong

excess spread: chill dough, use cool trays and weigh flour; tough: shorten flour mixing; crisp not chewy: remove earlier at set edges, allowing carry-over cooking.

Vanilla butter cake

Room-temperature butter, eggs and milk help this small cake batter emulsify evenly.

Makes one 20 cm round cake, 8 slices

Ingredients & method — Vanilla butter cake

Allow time

1½–2 hours, including baking and full cooling

Bowl: 4.7 L stainless steel bowl

Equipment: Mixer, flex-edge beater, scales, 20 cm round tin and cooling rack

The ingredients

  • soft unsalted butter 85 g
  • caster sugar 200 g
  • beaten eggs 100 g
  • plain flour 160 g
  • baking powder 4 g
  • fine salt 1.4 g
  • whole milk 120 ml
  • vanilla extract 4 ml

The method

  1. Grease and line a 20 cm round tin. Heat oven 175°C conventional / 155°C fan. Combine butter/sugar 2 30–60 seconds; increase to 4–6 for 3–4 minutes until pale and fluffy, stopping to scrape.
  2. Add egg gradually on 4, about 1 minute total, then vanilla. Scrape.
  3. Sift flour, baking powder and salt. At Stir/2, alternate dry ingredients and milk in three additions; mix only until combined, roughly 30–60 seconds total. Hand-fold any flour pockets.
  4. Scrape into tin; bake 30–40 minutes until centre springs back and skewer has no wet batter. Cool in tin 10–15 minutes, then rack completely.

The endpoint: Centre springs back and a skewer has no wet batter.

If it goes wrong

curdling: match egg/milk/butter temperatures; a little measured flour from the recipe can help. Dense cake: cream thoroughly but do not overmix flour. Brown top/wet middle: tent loosely and continue baking, checking oven temperature.

Pancake batter

Small lumps are welcome: stop mixing before the batter becomes completely smooth.

Makes approximately 8 pancakes using 60 ml batter each, 3–4 portions

Ingredients & method — Pancake batter

Allow time

30–45 minutes, including 10–20 minutes batter rest

Bowl: 4.7 L stainless steel bowl

Equipment: Mixer, flex-edge beater, measuring jug, frying pan and stove

The ingredients

  • plain flour 150 g
  • caster sugar 20 g
  • baking powder 6 g
  • fine salt 1 g
  • milk 200 ml
  • beaten egg 50 g
  • neutral oil 15 ml
  • vanilla extract 5 ml
  • extra oil 5 ml for pan

The method

  1. flex-edge beater, Stir dry ingredients 10–15 seconds. Precombine milk, egg, oil and vanilla in a jug.
  2. Add liquids on Stir/2, 15–25 seconds only until no dry flour streaks; some small lumps should remain. Stop rather than beating smooth. Rest 10–20 minutes while heating pan.
  3. Lightly grease pan on medium heat. Ladle about 60 ml batter per pancake. Cook 2–3 minutes until bubbles form and edges look set; flip once and cook 1–2 minutes more until golden underneath and centre cooked. Do not press flat.

The endpoint: Bubbles and set edges before flipping; golden underside and cooked centre afterward.

If it goes wrong

rubbery: overmixed or pressed after flipping; burnt outside/raw centre: lower heat; too thick after resting: stir in 5–10 ml extra milk by hand.

Standard tools09 / 25

Basic crêpe batter

One thin, unsweetened batter for lemon and sugar, chocolate, or savoury fillings. No baking powder.

Makes Around 12 thin crêpes, depending on pan size and batter per crêpe

Ingredients & method — Basic crêpe batter

Allow time

About 10 minutes preparation, 30 minutes refrigerated resting and 15–20 minutes cooking

Bowl: 4.7 L stainless steel bowl

Equipment: KSM195, supplied flex-edge beater, jug, sieve, ladle, approximately 20 cm non-stick frying pan and thin spatula

The ingredients

  • 140 g plain flour
  • 200 ml whole milk
  • 100 ml water
  • 2 eggs (about 100 g without shells)
  • 25 g unsalted butter, melted and cooled slightly
  • A pinch of salt
  • A little extra butter for the pan

The method

  1. In a jug, lightly beat eggs with milk and water. Fit the flex-edge beater in the 4.7 L bowl while the mixer is off and unplugged. Add sifted flour and salt; lower and lock the head.
  2. On Stir, gradually pour in the liquid mixture, then use speed 2 briefly, about 30–60 seconds, just until smooth. Stop and scrape only while stopped. If small lumps remain, pass the batter through a sieve rather than beating it for several minutes. Do not use high-speed creaming or whipping.
  3. Cover and refrigerate the batter for 30 minutes. Add the slightly cooled melted butter and combine at Stir for 5–10 seconds, or stir by hand. The batter should pour like thin cream; if too thick to swirl, add water 5–10 ml at a time.
  4. Heat the pan over medium heat and very lightly grease with butter. For a roughly 20 cm pan, start with about 35–45 ml batter, immediately tilting and swirling to coat the base in a thin layer. Adjust the next crêpe to the pan.
  5. Cook about 45–60 seconds until the surface is set and the underside lightly golden. Loosen the edge and flip; cook another 20–30 seconds until lightly freckled with no wet batter remaining. Adjust heat if it colours before setting.
  6. Serve as you go, or stack with baking paper between crêpes. Add sweet or savoury fillings after cooking. Keep unused batter refrigerated at 4°C or below and use within 24 hours; do not taste raw batter.

The endpoint: Thin, pourable batter; cooked crêpes are flexible and lightly freckled, not thick or fluffy.

If it goes wrong

Rubbery: batter was overworked or crêpes overcooked. Thick: use less batter or thin with a little water. Sticking: let the pan warm properly, grease lightly and wait until the first side sets before flipping. The first crêpe is a useful test of pan heat and batter quantity.

Vanilla buttercream

Hold some cream back so the buttercream stays spreadable rather than runny.

Makes approximately 540 g, enough for 12 generously iced cupcakes or a modest coating on one 20 cm cake

Ingredients & method — Vanilla buttercream

Allow time

15–20 minutes; cake must already be completely cool

Bowl: 4.7 L stainless steel bowl

Equipment: Mixer, flex-edge beater, scales, sieve and spatula

The ingredients

  • softened unsalted butter 195 g
  • sifted pure icing sugar 300 g
  • pure cream 40 ml (hold some back)
  • vanilla extract 5 ml
  • fine salt 1 g

The method

  1. Beat butter 2 30 seconds, then 4–6 2–3 minutes until creamy and paler. Stop and scrape.
  2. Add icing sugar in three portions on Stir, roughly 30 seconds each until contained, then 4 2–3 minutes until light.
  3. Add vanilla, salt and cream gradually at 2, about 30 seconds; hold back liquid once spreadable. Beat 4 1 minute, then 2 1–2 minutes to reduce large air bubbles. Use only on fully cooled cake.

The endpoint: Light, smooth, spreadable frosting without large air bubbles.

If it goes wrong

gritty: sift and use pure icing sugar; too loose: chill briefly, then beat at low speed; too firm: add cream 5 ml at a time; bubbles: final slow paddle mixing. Contains dairy: refrigerate covered and soften gently before rebeating.

Makes about 0.68–0.75 L base, 0.75–0.9 L finished, 6 small servings

Ingredients & method — Strawberry sorbet

Allow time

5–8 hours after bowl is frozen, including 2–3 hours chilling and 2–4 hours hardening; freeze bowl ≥24 hours ahead

Bowl: Optional 5KSMICM freeze bowl replaces the normal mixing bowl for churning

Equipment: Compatible mixer and pre-frozen ice-cream bowl, correct dasher and drive, scales, measuring jug, silicone spatula and covered shallow freezer container; blender and sieve

The ingredients

  • Washed, hulled ripe strawberries to yield 600 ml smooth purée (buy about 700–800 g; exact requirement varies)
  • 125 g sugar
  • 5–10 ml lemon juice, to taste
  • 0.5 g salt

The method

  1. Freeze the compatible ice-cream bowl for at least 24 hours; follow the exact attachment manual if it requires longer. Use pasteurized dairy where specified.
  2. blend berries smooth, measure 600 ml purée, blend sugar in about 30 seconds until dissolved; add lemon and salt.
  3. Optional fine straining for fewer seeds (measure after straining if strict consistency is wanted).
  4. Chill 2–3 hours or longer to ≤4°C.
  5. Chill the base to 4°C or below. Measure its cooled volume: the maximum starting base is 1.4 L, not the 1.9 L finished capacity. Do not double this batch blindly.
  6. Stop and unplug to fit the frozen bowl, correct dasher and drive. Start the dasher on Stir / lowest speed before pouring in the cold base; never pour into a stationary dasher. Churn about 20–30 minutes until thick soft-serve with tracks and ridges. Stop immediately if the dasher stalls; do not increase speed.
  7. Pack into a cold covered shallow container and freeze at −18°C or colder 2–3 hours, allowing 4 hours if still soft until scoopable. Completely refreeze the bowl before another batch; allow it to reach room temperature before hand-washing and follow its generation-specific cleaning instructions.

The endpoint: Sugar grains dissolve completely; churned sorbet is thick and spoonable.

If it goes wrong

cold base tastes slightly sweeter/sharper than the desired frozen result. No crunchy sugar grains should remain. Hard/icy: underripe fruit or too little sugar; syrupy/slushy: excess sugar or insufficient freezing. Sugar is structural here, not just optional sweetness. Do not add lots of water to ease blending. Wash fruit; freezing raw fruit does not sanitize it. No raw-shell-egg 'float test' is recommended. If the base stays liquid after 20–30 minutes, stop and refrigerate it; check bowl freeze, base temperature, assembly and volume before retrying with a fully refrozen bowl. Never solve a stalled dasher with more speed or hot water.

Makes about 0.65–0.75 L base, 0.7–0.9 L finished, 6 small servings

Ingredients & method — Pistachio gelato-style ice cream

Allow time

7–10 hours after bowl is frozen, including ≥4 hours chilling and 2–4 hours hardening; freeze bowl ≥24 hours ahead

Bowl: Optional 5KSMICM freeze bowl replaces the normal mixing bowl for churning

Equipment: Compatible mixer and pre-frozen ice-cream bowl, correct dasher and drive, scales, measuring jug, silicone spatula and covered shallow freezer container; saucepan, whisk, fine sieve, thermometer and ice-water bath

The ingredients

  • 500 ml whole milk
  • 65 g sugar
  • 16 g cornstarch

  1. Freeze the compatible ice-cream bowl for at least 24 hours; follow the exact attachment manual if it requires longer. Use pasteurized dairy where specified.

  3. Heat remaining milk with sugar until almost boiling.
  4. Whisk in slurry and gently simmer 3 minutes, stirring continuously; it should lightly thicken, not scorch.

Whipped cream

Cold cream and chilled tools help you reach soft peaks before the cream starts separating.

Makes About 450–500 ml whipped cream; 4–6 servings

Ingredients & method — Whipped cream

Allow time

15–20 minutes, including 10 minutes chilling tools

Bowl: 2.8 L stainless steel bowl with wire whisk; 4.7 L bowl also available

Equipment: Mixer, wire whisk, bowl, refrigerator and spatula

The ingredients

  • 250 ml cold pasteurized pure or thickened whipping cream (about 35% fat)
  • 15 g icing sugar
  • 2 ml vanilla extract

The method

  1. Chill bowl and whisk for about 10 minutes. Add cold cream, icing sugar and vanilla.
  2. Start low, gradually increase to speed 8–10, and watch continuously. Stop at soft or medium peaks, usually within a few minutes; do not chase a fixed timer.
  3. Use promptly or refrigerate covered at 4°C or below; use within 2 days.

The endpoint: Cream holds soft drooping peaks or medium peaks without graininess.

If it goes wrong

If the cream stays liquid, check that it is whipping cream and properly cold. Stop as soon as peaks hold: grainy cream is overwhipped and continued whipping separates butter and liquid.

Homemade butter

Washing away the trapped sweet-cream liquid gives the butter a cleaner texture and flavour.

Makes Estimated 180–220 g butter, depending on cream fat and draining

Ingredients & method — Homemade butter

Allow time

20–30 minutes, including whipping, draining and washing

Bowl: 4.7 L stainless steel bowl

Equipment: Mixer, wire whisk, flex-edge beater, bowl, sieve or cheesecloth, spatula and ice-cold water

The ingredients

  • 500 ml pasteurized pure or thickened whipping cream (about 35% fat)
  • Ice-cold water, about 60 ml per wash, repeated as needed
  • Optional 2 g fine salt

The method

  1. Add cream to bowl. Start low and gradually increase to a whipping speed, using a compatible shield if needed. Whip past cream peaks until yellow butter solids separate from liquid, around 10 minutes but variable.
  2. Stop and drain liquid through a sieve or cheesecloth; press solids gently.
  3. Wash the drained butter by hand in a clean bowl: add about 60 ml ice-cold water, press gently with a spatula, drain cloudy water and repeat until water stays clear. Press out water; do not force the wire whisk through firm butter.
  4. Stop and unplug to fit the flex-edge beater. Add optional salt and mix briefly on low just until combined. Wrap and refrigerate at 4°C or below.

The endpoint: Distinct yellow butter solids separate from liquid; final wash water stays clear.

If it goes wrong

If separation is slow, continue watching rather than assuming a fixed whipping time. Cloudy washing water means more liquid remains trapped; wash again and press out water. Stop before draining or scraping and do not let the machine strain against firm butter.

Mashed potatoes

Add warm milk gradually and stop early to avoid turning the potatoes gluey.

Makes About 900–950 g; 4 side servings

Ingredients & method — Mashed potatoes

Allow time

35–45 minutes, including separately cooking potatoes

Bowl: 4.7 L stainless steel bowl

Equipment: Mixer, pastry beater, bowl, scales, saucepan, stove and colander

The ingredients

  • 750 g potatoes, peeled
  • 100–150 ml warm milk
  • 40 g butter
  • 4 g fine salt
  • Water for boiling

The method

  1. Cut potatoes into even pieces, cover with water in a saucepan, bring to a boil and simmer until completely tender, about 15–25 minutes depending on size. The mixer does not cook them.
  2. Drain thoroughly, allow steam to escape briefly and warm the milk separately.
  3. Fit pastry beater. Put hot cooked potatoes and butter into bowl, use speed 2, and gradually add 100 ml warm milk plus salt. Add up to 50 ml more milk only as needed.
  4. Stop as soon as smooth or at your preferred texture; scrape only while stopped. Serve hot or cool promptly in shallow containers and refrigerate within 2 hours, or 1 hour above 32°C.

The endpoint: Tender potatoes crush easily; mash is soft with no hard pieces, not elastic or gluey.

If it goes wrong

Hard lumps mean the potatoes were undercooked; cook them thoroughly before mixing. Excess milk makes loose mash, so hold some back. Gluey mash comes from overworking starch and cannot be fixed by further beating.

Shredded cooked chicken

A short low-speed paddle run shreds warm cooked chicken without turning it into fine crumbs.

Makes 400 g shredded cooked chicken; 3–4 servings

Ingredients & method — Shredded cooked chicken

Allow time

5 minutes from fully cooked chicken; cooking time is additional and depends on the separate method

Bowl: 4.7 L stainless steel bowl

Equipment: Mixer, pastry beater, bowl, separate cooking appliance and food thermometer

The ingredients

  • 400 g fully cooked, boneless, skinless chicken

The method

  1. Cook chicken separately by a suitable method and verify at least 74°C in the thickest part with a food thermometer before it enters the mixer. Begin with 400 g fully cooked boneless, skinless chicken, not 400 g raw chicken.
  2. Fit the supplied pastry beater, not the flex-edge beater. Put warm cooked chicken into the bowl.
  3. Start briefly at speed 2, then use speed 4 for shredding as specified in the KSM195 manual. Check after 10–15 seconds and stop when shreds are the size you want; total time varies, so do not run for a fixed minute. Check texture while stopped. Add any sauce afterward to reduce splatter.
  4. Serve promptly or refrigerate in shallow covered containers within 2 hours, or 1 hour above 32°C; keep at 4°C or below.

The endpoint: Chicken separates into distinct shreds, not paste; a thermometer has confirmed 74°C before shredding.

If it goes wrong

If the pieces are becoming too fine, stop sooner; further mixing will not restore long shreds. Remove all bones and skin before mixing. The mixer neither cooks nor sanitizes raw chicken, so never use shredding as a substitute for reaching 74°C.

Six useful interventions

Stop. Look. Adjust.

The dough fights back.

Cover and rest it 10–20 minutes before stretching. Elasticity is not dryness; more flour can make a tight dough tighter.

The mixer strains.

Stop immediately. Check load, stiffness, assembly and the model manual. Hand-finish or reduce the batch; never force a stalled machine.

The cake is tough.

Cream thoroughly before adding flour; mix briefly afterward. The flex-edge beater can overwork flour just as a rigid paddle can; stop once incorporated.

The pasta tears.

Rest covered. Start at the widest setting, fold until smooth and narrow one setting at a time. Light dusting, not a coat of flour.

The ice cream stays liquid.

Refrigerate the base and fully refreeze the bowl. Check assembly, cold-base temperature and volume. Faster churning will not create a colder bowl.

The sorbet freezes solid.

Sugar controls freezing as well as sweetness. Don’t casually reduce it. Temper before scooping; ripe fruit and measured purée make a difference.

Care & food safety

What the motor can’t fix.

Raw does not mean ready.

Do not taste raw flour, dough or batter—even egg-free dough or freshly milled flour. Freezing does not make raw eggs safe. Use pasteurized dairy and cook custard properly.

Cold chain, clean tools.

Keep dairy, fillings and raw meat at 4°C or below. Cook chicken to 74°C; ground beef, pork and lamb to 71°C. Refrigerate perishables within 2 hours, or 1 hour above 32°C.

Cleaning is attachment-specific.

Stop and unplug before tool changes, clearing jams or cleaning. Use supplied pushers. Classic pasta rollers: dry and brush, don’t immerse. Hand-wash metal grinder parts; follow the exact manual for every other part.

KSM195 cleaning: hand-wash the supplied wire whisk—the linked manual reserves dishwasher-safe wire whisks for other named models. Its bowls, pouring shield, coated dough hook, flex-edge and pastry beaters may be dishwasher-washed per that manual; follow the instructions for your actual parts. Never put the ordinary mixer bowls on a stovetop or in an oven or microwave. Keep hands, utensils, hair and clothing away from moving parts. Let a frozen churn bowl reach room temperature before washing; never thaw it under hot water. FDA temperature advice is a practical reference; follow stricter local instructions.
