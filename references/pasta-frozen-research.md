# KitchenAid pasta + frozen-dessert recipe research

**Status:** source-informed draft recipes for a home cooking webpage; not kitchen-tested by us. Metric quantities are deliberately conservative. Yields are approximate and vary with trimming, rolling thickness and air incorporation. Published recipe formulas, scaled formulas and editorial adaptations are identified below. Use the owner's manual for the exact mixer AND attachment generation; do not copy speeds between unrelated attachments.

## Verified attachment rules (important for page copy)

### Pasta roller/cutter: distinguish mixer speed from thickness dial
- Official KitchenAid 3-piece roller/cutter product tips specify **stand-mixer speed 2 for the pasta roller, speed 5 for the 6.5 mm fettuccine cutter, and speed 7 for the 2 mm spaghetti cutter**. These are attachment-specific, not a generic 'pasta speed'. [Official product tips](https://www.kitchenaid.com/countertop-appliances/product-tips/mixer-attachments/pasta-cutter-and-roller).
- KSMPRA-family manual covers KSMPRA, KSMPSA, KSMPCA, KSMFETPRA and KSMPDX: roller 2; lasagnette cutter 3; fettuccine cutter 5; spaghetti and capellini cutters 7. Do not apply these settings to the Gourmet Pasta Press or third-party cutters. [Official manual](https://api.whirlpoolcentral.ca/_resources/documents/KSMPRA_use_and_care_en.pdf).
- Roller **thickness dial** 1 is the widest starting gap. KitchenAid recommends dial 4–5 for lasagne, fettuccine and ravioli; 6–7 for particularly thin fettuccine. Start at 1, fold and repeat until smooth, then narrow progressively without further folding. Mixer speed remains 2. [Official roller chart](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Stand_Mixer_Attachments/Pasta_and_Grains/Pasta_Sheet_Roller/Using_the_Pasta_Sheet_Roller).
- Stop and unplug before fitting/changing attachments. Secure the hub knob; keep fingers, utensils, hair, necklaces, scarves and loose sleeves away. Never force an oversized lump through the roller.
- Roller/cutters: let dough debris dry about 1 hour, brush out, wipe with a dry cloth. **Do not immerse, put in the dishwasher, run a cloth through rollers, or scrape with a knife.** Manufacturer cleaning rules differ from those for the mixing bowl/hook.

### Ice-cream bowl: verified, with source discrepancy preserved
- KitchenAid 2022-and-after Product Help explicitly requires a **minimum 16-hour prefreeze**, ideally in the coldest rear of the freezer; the bowl must be completely frozen. Current US KSMICM product copy instead says **allow 24 hours**. Safe webpage planning instruction: **freeze at least 24 hours; follow your exact attachment manual if it requires longer**. Do not say 'up to 16 hours'. [Official tips](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Stand_Mixer_Attachments/Sweets_and_Desserts/Ice_Cream_Maker/2022_and_After/Tips_for_Great_Results), [current US KSMICM](https://www.kitchenaid.com/countertop-appliances/stand-mixers/attachments/p.ice-cream-maker-attachment.KSMICM.html).
- Fully assemble the bowl, dasher and drive first. **Start the dasher on STIR / the lowest speed before pouring in the cold base**. Product Help also calls this 'speed one'. Never add base to a stationary dasher: liquid can freeze instantly against the wall and lock/break the attachment. Churn roughly **20–30 minutes**, judging texture rather than a guaranteed timer. Faster mixer speeds are not a shortcut. [Making ice cream](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Stand_Mixer_Attachments/Sweets_and_Desserts/Ice_Cream_Maker/2022_and_After/Making_an_Ice_Cream).
- Rated capacity **1.9 L / 2 US quarts is finished ice cream, not starting liquid**. Official maximum starting base is **1.4 L** because volume expands. All cards below target about 0.6–0.9 L starting base. Measure actual cooled volume; do not double batches blindly. [Official tips](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Stand_Mixer_Attachments/Sweets_and_Desserts/Ice_Cream_Maker/2022_and_After/Tips_for_Great_Results), [UK 5KSMICM capacity](https://www.kitchenaid.co.uk/mixer-attachments/859711690400/ice-cream-maker-5ksmicm-white).
- Chilled means refrigerator-cold, about **4°C or below**, not merely room temperature. Cooked bases should be cooled promptly in an ice-water bath, then refrigerated. Churn to soft-serve; pack into a cold covered freezer container and ripen **2–4 hours**. A freezer at **−18°C or colder** is the normal home storage target. Overnight storage often requires brief tempering before scooping.
- Bowl is not a universal power-hub accessory. **US current KSMICM page** excludes tilt-head Artisan Mini **KSM3316/KSM3317** and bowl-lift **K5SS/KSM50/KSM500/KSM450**. A separate official attachment guide additionally lists **KSM5** as excluded, so older model owners should confirm with KitchenAid rather than rely on an exhaustive universal list. [US attachment guide](https://www.kitchenaid.com/content/dam/kitchenaid/en_us/attachments/KitchenAid%20Attachment%20Guide-new.pdf).
- **UK 5KSMICM page** excludes tilt-head **5KSM3310X/5KSM3311X** and bowl-lift **5KPM5 Heavy Duty/5KPM50/5K5**. Older **5KICA0WH** documentation has different exclusions/cleaning instructions; identify the exact attachment generation and country. Do not assert 'fits every KitchenAid'.
- Use frozen bowl promptly after removal. Do not thaw it under hot water; allow it to reach room temperature before hand washing. Dasher/drive dishwasher guidance varies by generation: obey its manual rather than promising all parts are dishwasher-safe. Re-freeze the bowl completely before another batch.

## Shared food-safety rules
- Raw flour is not ready-to-eat: **do not taste uncooked pasta dough**, including egg-free dough. Cook pasta; wash hands and surfaces after flour/egg handling. [FDA flour safety](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/handling-flour-safely-what-you-need-know).
- Use pasteurized milk, cream and ricotta. Cook egg custard; FDA describes **71°C / 160°F** as a Salmonella-killing internal temperature for its cooked ice-cream base. Our vanilla method targets **77–82°C** for custard thickening, without boiling. Prefer pasteurized yolks, especially for vulnerable diners. Freezing does not make raw eggs safe. [FDA homemade ice cream](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/enjoying-homemade-ice-cream-without-risk-salmonella-infection).
- Keep dairy, eggs, fillings and chilled bases refrigerated at ≤4°C; keep cumulative room-temperature handling short (under 2 hours; under 1 hour in very hot conditions). Wash fruit before hulling/zesting. Do not use visibly spoiled fruit. A sorbet's acidity and freezer are not sanitizers.
- Practical quality targets, not validated shelf-life promises: churn bases within 24 hours of preparation; cook/refrigerate fresh pasta promptly and use refrigerated dough or filled ravioli by next day, otherwise freeze. Enjoy homemade frozen desserts within 1–2 weeks for best texture. Use clean containers, label allergens and date. Frozen desserts contain milk where stated; egg pasta contains wheat/egg; pistachio contains nuts/milk. Sorbets and semolina dough are egg-free, but cross-contact matters for allergy claims.

## 1. Egg pasta sheets and hand-cut tagliatelle

**Formula:** editorial home-batch formulation using the classic approximately 100 g flour : 50 g whole egg ratio, with official KitchenAid mixing/rolling technique. Not an exact reproduction of its manual's larger flour-measured recipe.

**Ingredients:** 300 g plain/all-purpose flour or pasta-grade tipo 00; 150 g beaten whole egg without shells (about 3 medium-to-large eggs, weigh rather than assume); 0–15 g water only as needed; optional 1 g fine salt; 15–25 g additional flour for dusting (not automatically mixed into dough).

**Yield:** about 450–465 g dough; 3 generous or 4 moderate main-course portions; sheets or ribbons as required.

**Equipment:** mixer bowl, flat beater, dough hook; compatible KitchenAid pasta roller; scales, wrap/container, bench scraper, sharp knife and board, dusted tray/rack, large saucepan. Fettuccine cutter is optional, but produces 6.5 mm fettuccine, not an attachment expressly named tagliatelle.

**Method:**
1. Put flour and optional salt in bowl. Fit flat beater; mix at **speed 2 about 30 seconds**, gradually adding egg. Stop and scrape as needed. Squeezed crumbs should hold together; add water **2–5 g at a time** only if they will not cohere.
2. Stop, exchange for dough hook, knead **speed 2 for about 2 minutes**. A stiff pasta dough may not form a perfect mixer ball. Gather and finish by hand briefly if necessary rather than running a struggling motor longer. Aim for firm, pliable dough, not wet/sticky bread dough.
3. Wrap and rest **30 minutes** at cool room temperature. Keep unused quarters covered. Divide into four pieces; flatten each by hand.
4. Roller at mixer **speed 2**, thickness dial **1**: pass, fold in half and pass again about 3 times, until smooth and cohesive. Dust very lightly. Then pass progressively at dials **2, 3, 4, 5**, without folding. Dial **4–5** is the official sheet/fettuccine range. Use 5 for this starting recipe; thickness and tenderness are the cues, not a universal millimetre claim.
5. **Sheets:** cut to pan size, keep covered until cooking or filling. **Tagliatelle:** let sheets become touch-dry **5–10 minutes**, still flexible, dust, loosely fold and cut by hand into **7–8 mm ribbons**, then uncoil. Alternative: fit the 6.5 mm fettuccine cutter while unplugged and run at **speed 5**; label result fettuccine.
6. Boil ribbons in generously salted water; check after **60–90 seconds**, usually **2–4 minutes** depending on thickness. Bite should be tender with a slight resistance, not a raw floury core. Sheets used in lasagne need a separate assembly/baking recipe; do not imply the sheet-making card is a complete safe baked dish.

**Troubleshooting:** cracks or tearing: hydrate sparingly and rest, avoid jumping thin settings; shrinking: cover and rest another 10 minutes; sticking: dust lightly and let surface dry briefly; cutters fail to separate: sheets too wet or too thick, allow touch-drying. Do not keep adding flour until dough is brittle. Refrigerate promptly or freeze dusted nests individually before bagging.

**Sources:** [KitchenAid perfect dough and speed 2 paddle](https://www.kitchenaid.co.uk/faq/making-perfect-pasta-dough); [official mix/hook sequence + hand ravioli](https://www.kitchenaid.co.uk/mixers/pasta-makers); [official rest/rolling/cooking](https://www.kitchenaid.co.uk/blog/how-to-make-homemade-pasta); attachment sources above.

## 2. Egg-free semolina pasta

**Formula:** ¾ of pasta specialist Gabriella Simonian's 400 g flour / 200 ml water recipe; mixer-assisted initial mixing is editorial and follows KitchenAid's conservative pasta sequence rather than the source's hand kneading.

**Ingredients:** 300 g **fine semola rimacinata di grano duro** (finely remilled durum semolina, not coarse semolina pudding grains); 150 g warm water; extra 10–20 g semolina for dusting; optional 1 g fine salt.

**Yield:** about 450 g dough, 3–4 portions. Best suited to hand shapes; roller ribbons are an option, not a promise that every coarse semolina flour will roll well.

**Equipment:** bowl, flat beater and hook, scraper, board; optional roller and knife; pot/tray.

**Method:** mix flour and water with flat beater **speed 2 about 30 seconds**, stop, gather, then hook **speed 2 about 2 minutes**. Finish by hand until cohesive and smooth (roughly **5–8 additional minutes** as needed), stopping machine use if it strains. Cover and rest **30–45 minutes** to hydrate fully. Shape small cavatelli/orecchiette by hand, or divide into quarters and roll at **mixer speed 2**, starting dial 1, folding until smooth, then progressively to **4–5**; hand-cut ribbons. Cook small shapes/ribbons in salted boiling water, begin checking at **2–3 minutes**; thick hand shapes may need **4–6 minutes or longer**. Cook until no hard/raw centre remains.

**Cues/troubleshooting:** dough feels relatively firm at first and becomes supple with rest. Sandy cracking: use finer flour, longer covered rest, or knead with wet hands; do not pour in large extra quantities of water. Sticky: tiny dusting of semolina. If it resists rolling, use hand shapes or rest longer rather than forcing it. Egg-free is still wheat-containing and raw flour is still unsafe to taste.

**Sources:** [pasta specialist, exact ratio/rest](https://noregrettispaghetti.co/semolina-pasta-dough/); [Nudo supporting 2:1 ratio](https://www.nudopastatools.com/recipes/semolinapastadough). Nudo suggests about 10 minutes machine kneading on unspecified low speed and says dough cannot be over-kneaded: **do not reproduce those as KitchenAid-specific motor guidance**.

## 3. Ricotta ravioli: rolled sheets, hand-filled and sealed

**Formula:** one egg-pasta batch from card 1; ricotta filling based on KitchenAid's 200 g ricotta / 25 g Parmesan filling, scaled 1½ times. Beetroot dough is deliberately not used.

**Ingredients:** 450 g egg pasta dough (card 1); 300 g pasteurized ricotta, well drained; 38 g finely grated Parmesan; finely grated zest of ½ lemon; 0.5 g black pepper; up to 1 g salt, adjusted for cheese; a little water to seal.

**Yield:** roughly **30–40 ravioli** at about 5 cm each; 3–4 mains. Count varies with sheet trimming and filling size.

**Equipment:** mixer/pasta roller; bowl, teaspoon or piping bag, pastry brush, pasta wheel/knife, dusted tray; no ravioli-making attachment required.

**Method:** keep ricotta cold; drain until it holds a mound without weeping. Mix filling by hand. Roll dough following card 1 at mixer **speed 2**, progressively to thickness dial **5**, the official KitchenAid ravioli setting range. Fill one workable sheet at a time, keeping the others covered. Place **about 7–9 g filling** per raviolo, leaving at least 1 cm sealing margin. Lightly dampen bare margins only if necessary. Cover with another sheet or fold; press out air from around each mound, then press margins firmly and cut. Do not flour sealing surfaces heavily. Cook promptly in gently boiling salted water **about 3–4 minutes**, checking a piece: pasta edges should be tender and filling hot. Floating alone is not proof of doneness. Refrigerate overnight at most or freeze on a tray until solid (about **1–2 hours**) then bag; cook from frozen, allowing extra time and checking centre rather than promising a fixed number.

**Troubleshooting/safety:** bursts: trapped air, excess filling or vigorous boil; leaking seams: flour on seal, dried sheets or over-wetting; watery centre: insufficiently drained ricotta. Keep filling cold while rolling, do not leave assembled trays out all afternoon. A raw meat filling would need a separate validated safe-cooking method and is not covered here.

**Sources:** [KitchenAid ricotta filling and hand-sealing method](https://www.kitchenaid.co.uk/recipes/valentines-day-pasta); [official hand ravioli guidance](https://www.kitchenaid.co.uk/mixers/pasta-makers); [official overnight refrigeration / tray freezing](https://www.kitchenaid.co.uk/faq/making-ravioli-with-ravioli-maker).

## Frozen-dessert equipment + common finishing sequence

For all five frozen cards: compatible mixer plus its own pre-frozen ice-cream bowl, correct dasher/drive, scales, measuring jug, silicone spatula, covered shallow freezer container. Cooked recipes additionally need saucepan, whisk, fine sieve, thermometer and ice-water bath; fruit recipes need a blender and sieve. **Do not use the mixer wire whisk to churn.** Prefreeze bowl ≥24 hours as explained above. Chill base thoroughly to ≤4°C, assemble, start on **STIR / lowest speed first**, pour in measured cold base, churn **20–30 minutes** until thick soft-serve with tracks/ridges. Stop when ready; do not keep churning against a stalled dasher. Pack and harden **2–4 hours** at −18°C. Yield depends on overrun; no commercial texture is guaranteed.

## 4. Vanilla custard ice cream

**Formula:** 80% of David Lebovitz's public vanilla recipe, using extract rather than a full vanilla-bean infusion; cooling/safety and KitchenAid steps added.

**Ingredients:** 200 ml whole milk; 400 ml double/heavy cream, ideally about 35% fat; 120 g sugar; 4 large egg yolks (about 65–75 g); 5 ml vanilla extract (or ½ split vanilla pod plus a little extract); 0.5 g salt.

**Yield:** roughly 0.75 L cold base, **0.85–1.0 L finished**, 6–8 small servings.

**Method:** heat milk, sugar and salt until steaming and dissolved. If using pod, scrape seeds in, cover off heat and infuse **30–60 minutes**, then rewarm. Whisk yolks in a bowl; gradually whisk in a little warm milk, then return to saucepan. Stir/scrape continuously on gentle heat to **77–82°C**, until custard coats the spatula and a finger-drawn line stays clear; do not boil. Strain immediately into the cream, stir in vanilla and cool in an ice bath. Refrigerate **at least 4 hours, preferably overnight** until ≤4°C. Remove pod. Follow the common **STIR, 20–30-minute churn**; harden **2–4 hours**.

**Troubleshooting/safety:** scrambled flecks mean overheating; immediate fine straining may help small flecks but a severely curdled/scorched batch will not become smooth ice cream. Do not use a spoon-coating cue as a replacement for thermometer safety. Icy: insufficiently frozen bowl/warm base or reduced sugar/fat; butter flecks: overchurning very fatty cream. Use pasteurized dairy and preferably yolks.

**Sources:** [Lebovitz exact starting formula and method](https://www.davidlebovitz.com/vanilla-ice-cream/); [King Arthur custard temperature 170–180°F](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/recipes/vanilla-bean-ice-cream-recipe); FDA and KitchenAid references above.

## 5. No-egg chocolate ice cream

**Formula:** approximately ¾ of King Arthur's Chocolate Decadence recipe, rounded to practical grams; **alcohol omitted**, so temper before scooping. This is milk/chocolate-based ice cream, not dairy-free, and not a no-churn recipe.

**Ingredients:** 32 g Dutch-process unsweetened cocoa; 149 g sugar; 19 g cornstarch; 0.8 g salt; 170 g water; 341 g whole milk; 128 g chopped semisweet chocolate; optional 5 ml vanilla extract.

**Yield:** roughly **0.75–0.85 L base**, about **0.7–0.9 L dense finished ice cream**, 6–8 small servings; the source has low overrun.

**Method:** whisk cocoa, sugar, starch and salt in saucepan. Gradually whisk in cold water and milk until smooth. Heat with frequent stirring, then near-constant stirring as hot, until the mixture **comes to a boil and thickens**. Remove from heat, add chocolate and stir until glossy and fully melted; add vanilla. Cool promptly in an ice bath, cover and chill **4 hours or overnight**. Stir the cold thick base smooth and verify volume. Follow common **STIR 20–30-minute churn**, pack and harden **3–4 hours**.

**Troubleshooting/safety:** lumps: slurry was incompletely dispersed; sieve while still warm. Scorching: insufficient scraping or excessive heat; burned flavour cannot be strained away. Very hard after overnight freezing is expected with this alcohol-free source adaptation; temper **10–20 minutes**, longer if needed, rather than cut sugar radically. Use pasteurized milk; check chocolate allergens. Cornstarch is not instant ClearJel and must be cooked.

**Source:** [King Arthur Chocolate Decadence, egg-free public recipe](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/recipes/chocolate-decadence-ice-cream-recipe).

## 6. Strawberry sorbet

**Formula:** scaled from Max Falkowitz's Serious Eats ratio **one quarter cup sugar per cup berry purée**, converted to a practical metric starting formula. Measure purée volume; fruit ripeness can change texture.

**Ingredients:** enough washed, hulled ripe strawberries to yield **600 ml smooth purée** (buy about 700–800 g; exact requirement varies); **125 g sugar**; 5–10 ml lemon juice, to taste; 0.5 g salt.

**Yield:** about **0.68–0.75 L base**, **0.75–0.9 L finished**, 6 small servings.

**Method:** blend berries smooth, measure **600 ml purée**, blend sugar in **about 30 seconds** until dissolved; add lemon and salt. Optional fine straining for fewer seeds (measure after straining if strict consistency is wanted). Chill **2–3 hours or longer** to ≤4°C. Follow common **STIR 20–30-minute churn**; pack and harden **2–3 hours**, allowing 4 if still soft.

**Cues/troubleshooting/safety:** cold base tastes slightly sweeter/sharper than the desired frozen result. No crunchy sugar grains should remain. Hard/icy: underripe fruit or too little sugar; syrupy/slushy: excess sugar or insufficient freezing. Sugar is structural here, not just optional sweetness. Do not add lots of water to ease blending. Wash fruit; freezing raw fruit does not sanitize it. No raw-shell-egg 'float test' is recommended.

**Sources:** [Serious Eats strawberry recipe](https://www.seriouseats.com/quick-easy-strawberry-sorbet-recipe); [sorbet science, fruit/sugar variability](https://www.seriouseats.com/how-to-make-the-best-sorbet).

## 7. Lemon sorbet

**Formula:** BBC Food's public churned formula, with juice volume rather than a fixed count of variable-sized lemons.

**Ingredients:** 225 g caster sugar; **350 ml water total** (250 ml syrup + 100 ml cold water); **225 ml freshly squeezed lemon juice**, strained; finely grated yellow zest of 2–3 washed unwaxed lemons (no white pith). No alcohol or raw egg.

**Yield:** roughly **0.7–0.75 L base**, **0.75–0.9 L finished**, 6 small servings.

**Method:** slowly heat sugar with **250 ml water**, stir until dissolved, boil **2 minutes**, remove from heat. Add zest and lemon juice; cool promptly. Refrigerate **at least 2 hours**, longer until ≤4°C. Add remaining **100 ml cold water**; sieve if a smooth zest-free texture is wanted. Follow common **STIR 20–30-minute churn** and harden **2–4 hours**.

**Cues/troubleshooting:** clear dissolved syrup, strongly tart base, thick spoonable slush after churn; lemon lacks berry pulp and naturally has a lighter/icier texture. Bitter: white pith or excessive peel; hard: under-sugared or very cold storage; slushy: inaccurate water/sugar or warm bowl. Do not use the berry sugar ratio indiscriminately for tart citrus. Temper a few minutes before serving. Rinse citrus before zesting.

**Source:** [BBC Food lemon sorbet, official public formula/method](https://www.bbc.co.uk/food/recipes/lemon_sorbet_44326). Alternative lower-sugar/tangier published formula is [Epicurious's Lebovitz lemon sorbet](https://www.epicurious.com/recipes/food/views/lemon-sorbet-379911), but do not mix the two formulas ingredient by ingredient.

## 8. Gelato-style pistachio (egg-free, milk-and-starch base)

**Formula:** David Lebovitz's public Sicilian-style starch recipe at its original conservative size. **Use the specified sweetened pistachio cream, not 100% nut paste**. 'Gelato-style' acknowledges a domestic KitchenAid churn is not commercial gelato equipment.

**Ingredients:** 500 ml whole milk; 65 g sugar; 16 g cornstarch; **200 g sweetened pistachio cream/paste containing approximately 40–50% pistachios plus sugar/fat**, as the source specifies; 2–3 drops lemon juice, optional.

**Yield:** about **0.65–0.75 L base**, **0.7–0.9 L finished**, 6 small servings.

**Method:** whisk starch into **60 ml of the milk** until smooth. Heat remaining milk with sugar until almost boiling. Whisk in slurry and gently simmer **3 minutes**, stirring continuously; it should lightly thicken, not scorch. Transfer to bowl, cool promptly in an ice bath, cover and refrigerate **4 hours or preferably overnight**. Whisk in pistachio cream and optional citrus until completely smooth and evenly coloured; keep ≤4°C (re-chill if necessary). Follow common **STIR 20–30-minute churn** and harden **2–4 hours**. Let briefly temper for a softer gelato-style service texture.

**Troubleshooting/safety:** gritty: nut product not finely ground or cream not dispersed; use smooth purchased cream rather than chopped nuts. Hard/bland: wrong product, especially substituting pure unsweetened paste without rebalancing sugar/fat. Sweetened spreads vary significantly; recipe needs practical testing with the selected brand. No validated one-for-one conversion to pure paste is supplied. Nut + milk allergens; inspect spread label for soy/other nuts. Natural colour is usually muted green/beige, not fluorescent green. Keep milk base cold.

**Source:** [Lebovitz pistachio gelato, starch method and essential paste note](https://www.davidlebovitz.com/pistachio-gelat/).

## Global troubleshooting box suitable for webpage

- **Ice cream remains liquid after 20–30 minutes:** stop, refrigerate base; confirm cold base, freezer/bowl freeze duration, correct assembly and measured batch volume. Do not solve it by increasing mixer speed. A partly thawed bowl needs a complete refreeze before retrying.
- **Dasher locks or machine strains:** stop immediately; never force or pry with mixer running. Check correct assembly, stationary-base freezing or over-churning against the model manual. Do not pour warm water into a frozen bowl to free it.
- **Icy after storage:** rapid chill, completely frozen bowl, shallow airtight storage and adequate recipe sugar/fat help; reducing sugar changes freezing behaviour. Prevent repeated thaw/refreeze cycles.
- **Very soft on leaving bowl:** expected; KitchenAid describes conversion as soft-serve and freezer ripening as 2–4 hours.
- **One bowl, five recipes:** sequential full batches cannot be made just by rinsing between flavours. Each requires a completely re-frozen bowl; prep on separate days or use additional bowls verified compatible.

## Editorial cautions to preserve

1. These are researched drafts, not our kitchen-tested or guaranteed recipes.
2. The **16 h vs 24 h official bowl-preparation discrepancy** is real; schedule 24 h and defer to exact manual.
3. Rated **1.9 L finished ≠ 1.9 L liquid**; manufacturer starting maximum is **1.4 L**.
4. The fettuccine cutter does not become a 'tagliatelle attachment'; hand cutting creates wider tagliatelle.
5. Pasta speeds are attachment-specific: roller 2 / fettuccine 5 / spaghetti 7 for the documented family only; dough hook stays 2.
6. Exact attachment generation and country matter for compatibility and cleaning. Some official excluded-model lists differ, including KSM5.
7. Pistachio **sweetened 40–50% cream and pure paste are not interchangeable**. This card preserves the source's ingredient specification and warns that brand differences warrant testing.
8. Source formulas support cooking methods, but adapted batch yields/time ranges are estimates, not measured results.
