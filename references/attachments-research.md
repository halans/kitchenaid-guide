# KitchenAid standard tools and optional attachments: practical findings

Sources are official KitchenAid US/Canada and FDA. Model examples identify equipment, not a guarantee of current local stock. Recipes below summarize official methods; use visual endpoints rather than promised times.

## What comes with the mixer—and what it cannot do alone

The usual bowl tools are **flat beater** (mix, cream, mash, shred cooked meat), **wire whip** (aerate cream/eggs), and **dough hook** (mix/knead yeast dough). Included tools vary by package: flex-edge and pastry beaters may be optional. Bowl tools must match the mixer/bowl model; a generic “fits every mixer” statement applies to the **power-hub attachments**, not all bowls or beaters. Knead yeast dough at **speed 2**, respecting the particular mixer's batch limit. A normal stand mixer does not cook food, boil pasta, freeze desserts, grind meat, roll pasta or juice produce without the relevant separate equipment/attachment. [KitchenAid tool guide](https://www.kitchenaid.com/pinch-of-help/stand-mixers/beater-attachments.html)

## Standard-tool mini recipes

- **Whipped cream:** wire whip + bowl; refrigerator and spatula. Chill bowl/whip about 10 minutes. Add **1 cup cold heavy whipping cream, 2 Tbsp powdered sugar, ½ tsp vanilla**. Start low, gradually increase to 8–10, and stop at desired soft/medium peaks. KitchenAid gives about 6–7 minutes, but observe continuously: overwhipping separates butter and liquid. Refrigerate leftovers; official recipe suggests up to two days. No specialty hub attachment required. [Official recipe](https://www.kitchenaid.com/pinch-of-help/stand-mixers/how-to-make-whipped-cream.html/)
- **Homemade butter:** wire whip + bowl; optional compatible pouring shield, fine-mesh sieve/cheesecloth, spatula and ice-cold water. Official batch: **4 cups heavy whipping cream** (check bowl capacity). Start low and increase gradually; whip past cream peaks until butter solids separate, roughly 10 minutes but variable. Stop, drain liquid and press solids in sieve. Return butter to clean bowl, wash with **¼ cup ice-cold water**, mix briefly (~10 seconds), drain cloudy water and repeat until clear. Use flat beater to mix salt/herbs only until combined; wrap and refrigerate. This is physical separation, **not pasteurization**; use pasteurized cream. The drained sweet-cream liquid is not automatically equivalent to cultured acidic commercial buttermilk in leavened recipes. [Official method](https://www.kitchenaid.com/countertop-appliances/pinch-of-help/how-to-make-butter)
- **Mashed potatoes:** flat beater + bowl; **pot/saucepan, stove and colander are also needed**. Official batch: **3 lb russet potatoes, ¾ cup half-and-half, 3 Tbsp unsalted butter, 1½ tsp salt, ¾ tsp pepper**. Cook potatoes until tender separately, drain, warm dairy separately. Add cooked potatoes/butter to bowl, begin speed 2, add warm dairy gradually, and stop as soon as desired texture is reached—overworking can make potatoes gluey. Wire whip is an optional finishing tool for a fluffy texture, not essential. [Official recipe and technique](https://www.kitchenaid.com/pinch-of-help/stand-mixers/how-to-make-mashed-potatoes.html)
- **Shredded chicken:** flat beater + bowl; separate cooking appliance and food thermometer. Put **fully cooked, boneless, skinless warm chicken** in bowl; shred on low roughly **30–60 seconds**, stopping earlier for larger pieces. Add sauce afterward to reduce splatter. KitchenAid does **not** recommend flex-edge beater for this job. Mixer does not cook or make raw chicken safe: poultry must reach **165°F/74°C** first. [KitchenAid method](https://www.kitchenaid.com/countertop-appliances/pinch-of-help/how-to-shred-chicken); [FDA temperatures](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/safe-food-handling)

These do not cook pasta or automatically fill/seal ravioli. Dough hook can prepare suitable dough first. Some regions also offer an integrated 3-in-1 roller/cutter; do not assume all sets have identical cutters. ## Safety and availability caveats to keep visible

1. **Read the exact mixer and attachment manuals.** Stop and unplug before installing/removing tools, scraping, clearing jams or cleaning; keep hands, utensils, loose hair and clothing away from moving parts. Use pushers where supplied. Do not generalize dough-hook speed 2 to hub attachments—their speeds differ. Classic roller/cutter cleaning instructions specify drying dough, brushing, no immersion/dishwasher and no cloth through rollers. [KitchenAid roller care](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Stand_Mixer_Attachments/Pasta_and_Grains/Pasta_Sheet_Roller/Cleaning_the_Pasta_Sheet_Roller_Attachment)
2. **Raw meat stays cold and separate:** hold at **≤40°F/4°C**, keep waiting batches refrigerated, grind/stuff promptly, then cook or refrigerate/freeze promptly. Chill/pre-freeze for texture is **not sterilization**. Wash hands for 20 seconds; disassemble grinder and clean food-contact parts with appropriate warm/hot soapy water, plus boards/counters, before switching to ready-to-eat food. Cook ground beef/pork/lamb/veal to **160°F/71°C**, ground poultry/all chicken to **165°F/74°C**. Refrigerate perishables within **2 hours**, or **1 hour above 90°F/32°C**; shallow containers cool leftovers faster. [FDA food handling](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/safe-food-handling)
3. **Do not blanket-label attachments dishwasher-safe.** KitchenAid pages conflict on metal grinder tray/collar dishwasher suitability; conservative guidance is **hand-wash metal parts and follow the exact supplied manual**, not a general blog. Current Product Help says all metal grinder parts hand-wash; plastic sausage parts/brush may be dishwasher-safe. Dry thoroughly. [Metal-grinder cleaning](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Stand_Mixer_Attachments/Grinders/Food_Grinder/Cleaning_the_Metal_Food_Grinder); [conflicting product-tips page](https://www.kitchenaid.com/countertop-appliances/product-tips/mixer-attachments/metal-food-grinder)
4. **Never taste raw flour, pasta dough, bread dough or batter**, including home-milled flour or egg-free dough. Milling does not remove bacteria; FDA also advises against home heat-treatment as a guarantee of safe-to-eat flour. Cook/bake as directed and clean flour-contaminated equipment/surfaces. [FDA flour safety](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/handling-flour-safely-what-you-need-know)
5. **Compatibility is not availability.** KitchenAid markets household power-hub attachments as fitting its household stand mixers, but region, legacy/current product version, included blades, sub-accessory compatibility, cleaning requirements and stock differ. A still-live product/help page does not prove currently sold locally. Check your country's catalog, mixer model, attachment SKU and included parts before buying. Do not imply a legacy juicer/processor/ravioli item is universally in stock. [US attachment overview](https://www.kitchenaid.com/countertop-appliances/stand-mixers/attachments); [Canada guide](https://www.kitchenaid.ca/en_ca/blog/s/attachment-buying-guide.html)
