Manual p. 5 explicitly says not to use speed 1 to mix or knead yeast dough. This takes precedence over the generic AU blog instruction to combine dough at speed 1. All four yeast recipes now use speed 2 for initial mixing and kneading. Hand-stirring yeast in a jug is not motor operation.

Manual pp. 5–6: pastry beater speed 2 for cutting butter and mashing; speed 4 for shredded meat. Recipe changes use the supplied pastry beater for shortcrust, mash and fully cooked chicken. Cake/cookie/frosting mixing uses the supplied flex-edge beater. Initial stiff pasta mixing is by hand before short hook work, avoiding any requirement for a separately purchased rigid flat beater.

Manual p. 9: supplied wire whip is hand-washed; dishwasher-safe whip exception is for 5KSM180/182, not KSM195. Bowls, pouring shield, coated hook, flex-edge and pastry beaters may be dishwasher-washed per this manual; actual supplied-part instructions govern. Never put ordinary mixer bowls on a stove, in an oven or in a microwave.

## Bowls and load

Current AU listing: 4.7 L and 2.8 L stainless steel bowls, wire whisk, flex-edge beater, pastry beater, hook and shield. The 2.8 L bowl page explicitly lists KSM195 compatibility; mixer product copy mentions small egg-white and frosting jobs. This page selects the smaller bowl only for its small whisk recipes, otherwise uses the main bowl. It does not promise flex-edge scraping or shield fit in the small bowl. No separate bowl adjustment procedure is invented.

AU heavy-dough guidance explicitly names KSM195: no more than 2 kg total heavy mixture including water, and 5–6 minutes running followed by 20 minutes rest. Guide conservatively uses at most five minutes, counts initial mixing and stops earlier for strain. The ceiling is not a scaling target. All formulations remain modest home batches, not machine-capacity test results.
