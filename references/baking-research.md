# KitchenAid stand-mixer baking research — Australian metric

## Editorial status and conventions
These are **research-based, conservative small-batch adaptations, not tested recipes**. Sources support the underlying recipes/techniques; adapted weights, yields, timings and troubleshooting below are editorial guidance, not claims that KitchenAid published these exact versions. Validate in a real kitchen before labelling anything tested. All eggs/whites are weighed **without shells**. Flour is plain unless bread flour is specified; use caster sugar, icing sugar and bicarbonate of soda terminology. Temperatures show **conventional / fan-forced °C**. Rise and bake times are estimates: use the stated endpoints. No specialist purchased attachments are required. Standard tools: flat beater, dough hook, wire whisk. Turn off and unplug before changing tools; stop before scraping. Secure bowl and lock tilt-head or raise bowl-lift bowl before starting. Start at Stir (usually 1) to avoid flour clouds and splashes.

## Verified operating cautions — display prominently
- **Knead yeast dough with the dough hook at speed 2; never increase above 2**, even for brioche. Australian official speed guidance says higher speed can damage the mixer. Speed 1 may combine ingredients; it is not a reason to keep kneading a struggling machine below the recommended speed.
- There is **no universal dough capacity or universal kneading time** for every KitchenAid model. Check the exact model's supplied manual, flour type and dough stiffness; bowl volume is not dough capacity. Do not scale these recipes automatically or treat an advertised maximum as a recommended everyday batch.
- Official AU heavy-dough guidance lists **2 kg total ingredients including water** for the tilt-head models it names (KSM45, KSM50, KSM156, KSM180, KSM192, KSM195), **3.6 kg for KSM60**, **3.8 kg for KSM70**, and **5–6 minutes heavy-mixing operation followed by 20 minutes rest**. These are source-specific examples, not certification for older, mini or different regional models; the exact manual takes precedence. Recipes below stay around or below 1 kg, but stiff dough can still overload a mixer.
- AU speed guide says typical kneading is 2–3 minutes. US product help explicitly notes flour, hydration and enrichment affect time, with enriched doughs often needing longer. Therefore **2–3 minutes is an initial check, not proof that all dough must be finished then**. Use short runs and rests/hand-finishing rather than extending continuous operation. Count preliminary heavy mixing toward the run limit. Brioche below deliberately uses separated runs.
- Stop if the machine strains, stalls, walks, knocks abnormally or develops an unusual smell; reduce load and consult the manual. Do not restrain a walking mixer or cure strain by increasing kneading speed. Soft/wet focaccia and brioche may remain attached to the bowl: do not keep adding flour merely to obtain a spotless bowl.
- Important source conflict: KitchenAid AU's 2020 `brioche-bread` recipe explicitly asks for speed 4 with a dough hook and long continuous kneading. **Do not reproduce that instruction**: the adaptation below uses speed 2 and breaks. Older focaccia/bread recipes also prescribe longer runs than the general 2–3-minute tip; endpoints and model operating limits govern.
- Raw flour and raw eggs are not ready-to-eat: do not taste raw dough/batter. Cook egg-containing recipes fully; do not present raw French meringue as ready-to-eat frosting. Cool breads/cakes before storing. Refrigerate cream-containing frosting and bring back to workable temperature before use.

## 1. Pizza dough
**Yield:** one 30–35 cm pizza, 3–4 portions. **Tool:** flat beater then dough hook. **Batch:** bread flour 300 g; instant dried yeast 7 g; caster sugar 2 g; fine salt 5 g; lukewarm water 150 ml; olive oil 50 ml; extra oil 5 ml for bowl. Approx. 510 g dough. Source's relatively oil-rich formula retained.

**Method / speed / endpoints:**
1. Flat beater, Stir, combine dry ingredients 15 seconds. Add water and oil on Stir; mix 30–60 seconds until shaggy and joined.
2. Stop, fit hook. Knead **2**, 2–3 minutes until smoother, elastic and slightly tacky. Stop earlier if already developed; do not chase a dry dough.
3. Oil bowl, cover; prove about 30–60 minutes until doubled (source's 15–30 minutes may be optimistic in a cool room).
4. Gently deflate by hand, stretch/roll to tray; rest 10 minutes if it springs back. Add modest toppings. Bake **230/210°C**, about 15–20 minutes, until crust is golden and base crisp and cooked. Cool 2 minutes before slicing.
**Troubleshooting:** no rise: check yeast age, water warmth and room temperature; tight dough: rest before stretching; soggy centre: reduce wet toppings and preheat oven fully. Do not put unsupported raw dough directly on oven bars.
**Authoritative recipe source:** https://kitchenaid.com.au/blogs/kitchenthusiast/gourmet-pizza-0

## 2. Sandwich bread (white tin loaf)
**Yield:** one approximately 21 × 11 × 7 cm loaf, 10–12 slices. **Tools:** flat beater, dough hook. **Batch:** bread flour 500 g; instant yeast 7 g; caster sugar 12 g; fine salt 5 g; lukewarm water 250–300 ml (start with 250); olive oil 60 ml; optional milk powder 15 g; bowl/tin oil 10 ml. Approximately 850–900 g dough. Adapted from the official white tin-loaf recipe; optional milk powder helps a softer sandwich crumb.

**Method:**
1. Combine dry ingredients on Stir 15 seconds. Add 250 ml water and oil, Stir 45–60 seconds, adding remaining water in 5–10 ml increments only if dry flour remains.
2. Fit hook; **2** for 2–3 minutes until smooth, springy and lightly tacky. If insufficiently developed, rest dough and mixer 20 minutes, then hook **2** another 1–2 minutes, or finish by hand. Observe exact model duty limit.
3. Cover in oiled bowl; prove 60–90 minutes until doubled. Deflate by hand; shape a tight log and put in greased tin.
4. Cover and prove 40–60 minutes until puffy and near the rim; a gentle finger dent should return slowly, not immediately.
5. Bake **200/180°C** for 25–35 minutes until golden, loaf sides firm and base sounds hollow. Turn out; cool completely, about 1–2 hours, before slicing. Brush warm crust with a little oil if a softer crust is desired.
**Troubleshooting:** dense loaf: allow fuller rise, do not add excess flour; collapsed top: reduce final proof; gummy slices: bake longer and cool fully.
**Source:** https://kitchenaid.com.au/blogs/kitchenthusiast/classic-white-crusty-bread-0

## 3. Rosemary focaccia
**Yield:** one approximately 20 × 30 cm tray, 6 portions. **Tools:** flat beater, dough hook. **Batch:** bread flour 400 g; instant yeast 6 g; salt 7 g; caster sugar 12 g; warm water 260 ml; olive oil 30 ml in dough + 20 ml for tin/top; rosemary leaves 2 g; sliced garlic 5 g; flaky salt 2 g. Approximately 710 g dough, excluding topping. Editorial simpler, wetter, unfilled adaptation of the official herb-and-garlic recipe (Parmesan filling omitted).

**Method:**
1. Flat beater, Stir dry ingredients 15 seconds; add water and 30 ml oil, Stir 45 seconds until joined.
2. Hook **2** for 2–3 minutes to a soft, stretchy dough; some sticking is normal. Cover and rest 20 minutes, then give one set of gentle stretch-and-folds by hand.
3. Cover in oiled bowl, prove about 60–90 minutes until doubled. Transfer to generously oiled tray; gently ease towards corners. Rest 10 minutes if resisting, then stretch again.
4. Cover 25–40 minutes until airy and puffy. Dimple with oiled fingers; distribute remaining oil, rosemary, garlic and flaky salt.
5. Bake **200/180°C** for 25–30 minutes until golden top and cooked, golden underside. Cool 10 minutes on rack.
**Troubleshooting:** sticky is expected; use oiled hands, not handfuls of extra flour. Tight stretching: rest. Pale wet base: use a metal tray and bake a little longer; shade exposed garlic if browning too quickly.
**Source:** https://kitchenaid.com.au/blogs/kitchenthusiast/herb-and-garlic-focaccia-0

## 4. Brioche — overnight small loaf
**Yield:** one small approximately 21 × 11 cm loaf, 8 slices. **Tools:** flat beater, dough hook. **Batch:** plain flour 275 g; milk 65 ml; caster sugar 15 g; instant yeast 3.5 g; beaten egg 100 g; soft but not melting unsalted butter 115 g; fine salt 3 g; additional beaten egg 15 g for glaze. Approximately 577 g dough. Half-size source adaptation with shell-free egg weights.

**Method:**
1. Stir milk, yeast and sugar together; wait 10 minutes if checking yeast activity. Flat beater, Stir in egg, flour and salt 45–60 seconds to a shaggy dough. Rest covered 20 minutes.
2. Hook **2** for 2 minutes. Add butter in three portions at **2**, allowing roughly 1–2 minutes per portion until absorbed. **Interrupt heavy mixing after at most 5 minutes including preliminary mixing, or sooner if the model manual requires; rest mixer 20 minutes before continuing.** Keep dough covered; refrigerate it during breaks if warm or greasy.
3. Continue in 2–3-minute runs at **2**, with required rests, until satiny and stretchy and a small piece stretches into a thin translucent membrane. Allow roughly 8–15 minutes total active hook time, not one continuous run. Hand-finish if necessary. Never use source's speed 4.
4. Cover, rise roughly 1–2 hours until doubled. Gently deflate/fold, cover and refrigerate at least 8 hours (overnight).
5. Divide cold dough into three equal balls or braid; place in buttered tin. Cover, prove approximately 2–3 hours until doubled/puffy; cold dough may take longer. Glaze lightly with egg.
6. Bake **170/150°C** 25–35 minutes until deeply golden and centre set; extend baking if needed and tent top if darkening. Cool in tin 5–10 minutes, then rack completely.
**Troubleshooting:** greasy dough: butter/dough too warm; chill briefly. Dough tears: use rest and further gentle development, not higher speed. Slow final proof: wait for puffiness rather than baking to the clock.
**Source (contains conflicting speed instruction, corrected above):** https://kitchenaid.com.au/blogs/kitchenthusiast/brioche-bread

## 5. Shortcrust pastry
**Yield:** pastry for one 23 cm single-crust tart, about 390–410 g. **Tool:** standard flat beater (optional pastry beater not required). **Batch:** plain flour 250 g; cold cubed unsalted butter 125 g; fine salt 2 g; ice-cold water 35–50 ml; optional caster sugar 10 g for sweet pastry. Editorial classic 2:1 flour:butter shortcrust; source supports flat-beater technique, not this exact formula.

**Method:**
1. Stir flour and salt/sugar 10 seconds. Add cold butter; **2** for 30–60 seconds, just until coarse crumbs with some pea-size butter pieces. Flat beater can take slightly longer than specialist pastry beater: watch rather than insist on a timer.
2. Stir while adding water in small increments, about 10–20 seconds total, stopping when a squeezed handful holds together. Finish pressing together by hand; do not knead smooth.
3. Flatten to disc, wrap and chill at least 60 minutes. Roll 3 mm thick, line tin without stretching; chill another 20–30 minutes.
4. For an unfilled shell, line with baking paper and baking weights. Bake **200/180°C**, 15–20 minutes; remove weights/paper and bake 8–12 minutes more until base dry and golden. For filled pies/tarts, follow filling recipe's baking instructions instead.
**Troubleshooting:** crumbly: add water 5 ml at a time; sticky/greasy: chill, not more vigorous mixing; shrinkage: avoid stretching and allow chilling. Never use dough hook for this pastry.
**Sources:** https://www.kitchenaid.com/countertop-appliances/pinch-of-help/how-to-make-homemade-pie-crust ; https://kitchenaid.com.au/blogs/kitchenthusiast/types-of-pastry

## 6. Chocolate chip cookies
**Yield:** approximately 12 cookies at 40 g dough each. **Tool:** flat beater. **Batch:** soft unsalted butter 100 g; brown sugar 75 g; caster sugar 62 g; vanilla extract 5 ml; beaten whole egg 25 g plus egg yolk 9 g; plain flour 138 g; bicarbonate of soda 1.2 g; fine salt 1 g; chocolate chips/chunks 100 g. Approximately 516 g dough. Half-size adaptation; yield recalculated from actual dough mass, not the source's inconsistent cookie count.

**Method:**
1. Combine butter/sugars on **2** 30 seconds, then **4** for 2–3 minutes until pale and creamy; stop and scrape midway.
2. Add egg/yolk and vanilla; **4** about 30–60 seconds until emulsified.
3. Add flour, bicarb and salt at Stir for 15–30 seconds, just until no flour streaks. Add chocolate at Stir 5–10 seconds or fold by hand.
4. Scoop 40 g portions; chill 20–30 minutes if soft. Place well apart on lined cool trays. Bake **180/160°C** 11–14 minutes until edges golden and centres still soft but not liquid. Rest on trays 5 minutes then rack.
**Troubleshooting:** excess spread: chill dough, use cool trays and weigh flour; tough: shorten flour mixing; crisp not chewy: remove earlier at set edges, allowing carry-over cooking.
**Source:** https://kitchenaid.com.au/blogs/kitchenthusiast/our-go-chewy-choc-chip-cookies-magdalena-roze

## 7. Vanilla butter cake
**Yield:** one 20 cm round cake, 8 slices. **Tool:** flat beater. **Batch:** soft unsalted butter 85 g; caster sugar 200 g; beaten eggs 100 g; plain flour 160 g; baking powder 4 g; fine salt 1.4 g; whole milk 120 ml; vanilla extract 4 ml. Approximately 775 g batter. Small-batch editorial adaptation: source's combined baking-powder/bicarb text is malformed and includes bicarb without an obvious acidic ingredient; **this draft deliberately uses 4 g baking powder only rather than silently reproducing that ambiguity**. The changed formula must be kitchen-validated before describing as tested.

**Method:**
1. Grease and line a 20 cm round tin. Heat oven **175/155°C**. Combine butter/sugar **2** 30–60 seconds; increase to **4–6** for 3–4 minutes until pale and fluffy, stopping to scrape.
2. Add egg gradually on **4**, about 1 minute total, then vanilla. Scrape.
3. Sift flour, baking powder and salt. At Stir/**2**, alternate dry ingredients and milk in three additions; mix only until combined, roughly 30–60 seconds total. Hand-fold any flour pockets.
4. Scrape into tin; bake 30–40 minutes until centre springs back and skewer has no wet batter. Cool in tin 10–15 minutes, then rack completely.
**Troubleshooting:** curdling: match egg/milk/butter temperatures; a little measured flour from the recipe can help. Dense cake: cream thoroughly but do not overmix flour. Brown top/wet middle: tent loosely and continue baking, checking oven temperature.
**Source:** https://kitchenaid.com.au/blogs/kitchenthusiast/vanilla-butter-cake

## 8. Pancake batter
**Yield:** approximately 8 pancakes using 60 ml batter each, 3–4 portions. **Tool:** flat beater. **Batch:** plain flour 150 g; caster sugar 20 g; baking powder 6 g; fine salt 1 g; milk 200 ml; beaten egg 50 g; neutral oil 15 ml; vanilla extract 5 ml; extra oil 5 ml for pan. Editorial small-batch formulation based on official ingredient list and mixer method; source does not provide quantities.

**Method:**
1. Flat beater, Stir dry ingredients 10–15 seconds. Precombine milk, egg, oil and vanilla in a jug.
2. Add liquids on Stir/**2**, 15–25 seconds only until no dry flour streaks; some small lumps should remain. Stop rather than beating smooth. Rest 10–20 minutes while heating pan.
3. Lightly grease pan on medium heat. Ladle about 60 ml batter per pancake. Cook 2–3 minutes until bubbles form and edges look set; flip once and cook 1–2 minutes more until golden underneath and centre cooked. Do not press flat.
**Troubleshooting:** rubbery: overmixed or pressed after flipping; burnt outside/raw centre: lower heat; too thick after resting: stir in 5–10 ml extra milk by hand.
**Source:** https://www.kitchenaid.com/countertop-appliances/pinch-of-help/how-to-make-homemade-pancakes

## 9. Vanilla buttercream
**Yield:** approximately 540 g, enough for 12 generously iced cupcakes or a modest coating on one 20 cm cake. **Tool:** flat beater. **Batch:** softened unsalted butter 195 g; sifted pure icing sugar 300 g; pure cream 40 ml (hold some back); vanilla extract 5 ml; fine salt 1 g. Source uses vanilla beans and 3 tablespoons cream; metric draft deliberately uses extract and a restrained 40 ml cream, adding to texture rather than converting ambiguous tablespoon conventions.

**Method:**
1. Beat butter **2** 30 seconds, then **4–6** 2–3 minutes until creamy and paler. Stop and scrape.
2. Add icing sugar in three portions on Stir, roughly 30 seconds each until contained, then **4** 2–3 minutes until light.
3. Add vanilla, salt and cream gradually at **2**, about 30 seconds; hold back liquid once spreadable. Beat **4** 1 minute, then **2** 1–2 minutes to reduce large air bubbles. Use only on fully cooled cake.
**Troubleshooting:** gritty: sift and use pure icing sugar; too loose: chill briefly, then beat at low speed; too firm: add cream 5 ml at a time; bubbles: final slow paddle mixing. Contains dairy: refrigerate covered and soften gently before rebeating.
**Source:** https://kitchenaid.com.au/blogs/kitchenthusiast/vanilla-cupcakes-kirsten-tibballs (Vanilla Cream Topping section)

## 10. Baked meringues
**Yield:** approximately 16 small meringues. **Tool:** wire whisk. **Batch:** egg whites 120 g (about 4 whites); caster sugar 240 g; white vinegar 3 ml; vanilla extract 5 ml; cornflour 2 g. Editorial all-caster-sugar adaptation at 2:1 sugar:white ratio; source uses mixed brown/caster sugar and a shorter, hotter bake.

**Method:**
1. Bowl and whisk must be scrupulously clean and grease-free. Whisk whites starting **2**, then **6** for 1–3 minutes until soft peaks (tips droop).
2. Add sugar gradually, about 15 g every 15–20 seconds on **6–8**, allowing roughly 4–6 minutes; whisk another 1–2 minutes if needed until stiff, glossy peaks and sugar feels dissolved when a little mixture is rubbed between clean fingers. Do not keep whipping once dry or clumpy.
3. Mix cornflour, vinegar and vanilla at Stir 5–10 seconds. Pipe/spoon 16 mounds onto lined trays.
4. Bake **110/90°C** for about 75–100 minutes until dry to touch and releasing easily from paper; time depends on size. Turn off oven and cool inside, door slightly ajar, about 1–2 hours. Store fully cooled in airtight container. This is a baked sweet, not raw topping.
**Troubleshooting:** whites will not whip: any yolk/fat contamination means restart with clean equipment; gritty/weeping: add sugar more slowly and dissolve fully; cracks: lower oven temperature and cool slowly; sticky after cooling: humidity or insufficient drying, dry further in low oven.
**Source for mixer technique:** https://kitchenaid.com.au/blogs/kitchenthusiast/petite-sugar-meringues-0

## Parent implementation notes
- Keep research/adaptation disclaimer and the speed-2 warning visible; do not bury inside recipe drawers.
- Exact model selector/manual link is preferable to a blanket “handles X kg” assertion. Small recipes here do not guarantee suitability for every model.
- Clearly separate **active mixing time** from **elapsed time**, especially brioche's 20-minute machine rests and overnight refrigeration.
- Cake, pancake, pastry and meringue drafts include explicit formulation changes or editorial quantities; do not describe their exact versions as official KitchenAid recipes. If only strict source reproductions are desired, replace those formulations with vetted metric recipes rather than concealing changes.
